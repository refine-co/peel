First, get the pack into Peel

Skip this if you made the pack yourself, or it came with the app — it is already there. This is for a pack you downloaded from this site.

1

Download it

Tap Download the sheet on the packs page. Your browser saves it to Downloads. Chrome may warn that the file type is unusual: it is a zip of pictures, and Peel is the only app that reads it.

2

Open it in Peel

Tap Open in Peel beside the download and Peel opens its file picker in your Downloads folder, with the pack right there. By hand works too: open Peel, tap New pack, scroll to the bottom of the sheet and tap Import a pack file.

3

Confirm

Peel shows what it found — “Peel Halloween, 20 stickers, will be added to your packs” — and checks every sticker before anything is added. The pack is now yours, on your phone. Everything below starts from here.

Drag this one up and to the left. That is the whole idea of the app, and the only thing on this page you can play with.

WhatsApp — the whole pack

Works with every Peel pack, still or animated.

1

Open the pack in Peel

Tap it on the home screen.

2

Tap Add to WhatsApp

The button sits at the bottom of the pack.

3

Confirm in WhatsApp

It appears in your sticker tray straight away.

Peel checks the pack against WhatsApp's rules before handing anything over — size, dimensions, the tray icon — and if one sticker is at fault it says which one, rather than letting WhatsApp refuse the lot.

Telegram — the whole pack

Works with still packs. Animated ones are not offered: Telegram wants WebM VP9 or TGS for those, which Peel does not make yet.

1

Have Telegram installed

The button only appears when Peel can see it. Telegram, Telegram X and Telegram Web all count.

2

Tap Add to Telegram

In the pack, next to the WhatsApp button.

3

Name the set

Telegram takes over from here: it asks for a name, publishes the set under your own account, and gives you a link you can share.

Your emoji tags travel with the stickers, so Telegram suggests each one the same way WhatsApp does.

TikTok, Instagram Stories, CapCut, Canva — one sticker

Video apps have no sticker packs. What they have is an overlay you pick from your gallery, so the sticker is saved to your phone first and placed from there.

1

Save the sticker

Open the pack in Peel, tap the sticker you want — press and hold, for an animated one — then tap Save to Photos. It lands in a Peel album: a still sticker as a transparent PNG, an animated one as a GIF.

2

Place it in the video app

In TikTok, start your video, tap the sticker icon down the right-hand side, and the first tile in that panel opens your gallery. Pick the sticker, tap OK, and it drops onto the video as an overlay you can drag and resize — cut-out edge intact, and an animated one keeps moving.

Instagram Stories, CapCut and Canva are the same idea: add an image or an overlay, then choose from the Peel album.

3

Say no to “Upload to remove background”

If TikTok offers it. Peel already cut the subject out on your phone; that option would send your picture to TikTok's servers to do it again.

Save to Photos arrives in the next Peel update, and it needs Android 10 or newer. Everything else on this page works with the version you have today.

If the button is not there

Tapping the download does nothing

Usually the browser rather than Peel: Chrome records the file as plain bytes and hands over a link with no filename, so Peel cannot tell it is a pack. Two ways round it, both one tap — Open in Peel beside the download, or Share from your Downloads list and pick Peel.

No Add to Telegram

Either Telegram is not installed, or the pack is animated. Still packs only, for now.

No Save to Photos

It arrives with the next update, and only on Android 10 and newer.

Why a file, and not a button inside the app

Peel has no internet permission at all. Not switched off — absent. You can see that for yourself in the app's permission list and in its Play Data safety entry. That is the whole point of Peel: your photos cannot be uploaded anywhere, because the app has no way to reach the network. The cost of that promise is this one: packs arrive as a file your browser downloads, rather than from inside the app.
