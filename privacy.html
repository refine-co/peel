Peel · privacy policy

Your photos stay on your phone

Peel turns your photos into WhatsApp sticker packs. The cutting-out happens on your phone. There is no account to create in order to use Peel, and no server receives your photos or your stickers — unless you choose to send a pack in, which is the one thing that uploads anything, and only the pack you picked.

Last updated 15 October 2026

The short version

Peel uses the internet for three things, and you ask for all of them. Tapping a pack or a scene downloads that file. Opening the More fun screen, or pulling it down, fetches the catalogue of what is available. Those two go to peel.refineco.app, over HTTPS. The third is sending a pack in, described below, which is the only thing Peel ever uploads and the only reason it has an account. Peel never does either on its own: nothing is fetched when the app starts, and somebody who never opens that screen and never taps a pack makes no request at all.

Neither can be redirected to another address. The download refuses anything that is not a pack or a scene and refuses a file over 8 MB; the list is one fixed address and is refused above 512 KB. The catalogue also ships inside the app, so the screen is full before any of this happens — the fetch only replaces it with whatever is published now.

Three things built by Google also use the network, and they are Google's, governed by Google's privacy policy rather than this one: the cut-out model that Play services may download the first time it is needed (described below), the Play Store's own rating sheet if it decides to appear, and Google Play Billing if you buy the unlock. None of them is given your photos or your stickers.

Your photos are never uploaded, and nothing you make is uploaded unless you send it in. The cutting-out happens on your phone. Peel does not sync, back up or copy your packs anywhere, and nobody can see what you make. The one exception is deliberate and takes several taps: choosing Send this to Peel on a pack you made, to offer it to other people.

If you send a pack in

This is the only part of Peel with an account, and you only meet it if you decide to share a pack. Making stickers, downloading packs and everything else works without one, and always will.

What is sent: the pack file you chose, the credit name you typed, and one optional line describing it. That is the whole list. No photos that are not in the pack, nothing about your phone, no identifier for your install.

Deleting your account. In Peel, Send this to Peel → Delete my account. It removes your email address, your name, every sign-in token, and any pack of yours still waiting to be looked at. It is immediate and it is not reversible.

A pack that has already been published is a separate thing, because other people have downloaded it. Ask us to take it down and we will remove it from the website and the catalogue, so nobody new can get it — but copies already on other people's phones stay there, and we have no way to reach them. We would rather say that plainly than imply a delete button we do not have.

There is no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Nobody, including us, can see what you make.

WhatsApp

When you tap Add to WhatsApp, WhatsApp reads that pack's stickers from Peel so it can install them. Only WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business are allowed to read them. Once stickers are in WhatsApp, and in any chat you send them to, WhatsApp's privacy policy applies to them, not ours.

The cut-out model

Peel uses Google's ML Kit to find the subject in a photo, and the analysis runs entirely on your phone. The model itself is supplied by Google Play services, a separate part of Android. The first time it is needed, Play services may download it, and that download is Google's, governed by Google's privacy policy. Your photo is never part of that download.

Deleting your data

Deleting a pack or sticker removes it from Peel. Uninstalling the app deletes everything Peel holds. Stickers already added to WhatsApp are managed inside WhatsApp.

Children

Peel is intended for people aged 13 and over.

Changes
